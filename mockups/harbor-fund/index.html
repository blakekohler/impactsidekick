Five design directions

Five very different takes on the homepage outlined below. Each one has the full homepage and a "Become a patron" page (use the button in its header), works on a phone, and uses your logo, photos and type. They're kept light so the pages stay fast.

1. The SlateYour black and off-white with Onest at poster scale, and a clapperboard stripe running through.
2. PremiereA dark theater: a letterboxed Forum reel, a film-strip slate, end credits and a ticket for patrons.
3. Festival ProgramA printed festival guide: condensed poster type, ruled listings and one cobalt accent.
4. Safe HarborDeep water, a lighthouse beam across the hero, and the evergreen fund drawn as a loop.
5. Patron MemoAn investor memo for philanthropists: numbers first, a capital-flow diagram and the slate as a portfolio.

Mock-ups only: the forms don't send anything, and film descriptions use only what's on your site or in the Variety piece.

The homepage we'd build

Your donors give large gifts, so a small-dollar Donate button isn't the main move. The page should lead three audiences to the right door: patrons, filmmakers and press. Here is the order, what each section says, and the one clear next step.

  1. Header

    Logo, then Portfolio · Film Slate · Film Forum · About · Contact. On the right, one button: Become a patron.

  2. Hero

    Your line stays: "Films that matter." Under it, one sentence on the model: a nonprofit, venture-style fund that puts philanthropic capital into film and TV for the public good and recycles returns into the next film. Primary button: Become a patron. Secondary link: Pitch a project.

  3. Proof strip

    Four numbers in a row: dollars raised, donors, projects funded, films released. We'd use the current figures from your team. Variety's July count was $15M, 82 donors and 22 projects.

  4. How it works

    Your seven-step model, cut to four: Source, Raise, Invest, Return. One line each, so a donor sees in ten seconds where the money goes and how it comes back to the fund.

  5. On the slate

    Three to six film cards with poster, logline and cause, for example Evicted, By Any Means and Valley of the Heart. Each links to its film page.

  6. The Harbor Film Forum

    Photos from Necker Island, Montana and Sundance, one line on who attends, and a button: Request an invitation.

  7. In the press

    Variety and Forbes headlines with a short pull quote each, linked to the articles.

  8. People

    Lindsay and the founding team, then the advisory board names. This is where trust is earned.

  9. Why film

    The Ebert line, "The movies are like a machine that generates empathy," as a full-width closing band over a still from one of your films.

  10. Footer

    Three doors (Patrons, Filmmakers, Press), a "Slate updates" email signup, the Salt Lake City address, Instagram and YouTube, and the current year.

The one clear next step for supporters

"Become a patron" opens a short form: name, email, and what they're interested in (giving, attending a Forum, or co-investing in a film). Each one goes straight to your team for a personal follow-up. Every section above points back to it.
